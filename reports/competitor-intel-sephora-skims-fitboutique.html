For Motion's SaaS context, Sephora's demo-led approach maps better (screen recordings, product walkthroughs).

SKIMS Headlines

"SKIMS [Collection]"
"SKIMS Swim"
"SKIMS Lightweight Cotton"
"SKIMS Easter Collection"

Pattern: Brand name + collection name. Every single headline starts with "SKIMS". Zero benefit language in headlines. Copy does the promotional work: "5 for $35 deal on new Lightweight Cotton panties!"

Copy Insight for Motion

Both mega-brands use extremely simple headlines. Sephora leads with novelty ("New!"), SKIMS leads with brand name. Neither uses question hooks, pain-point language, or long-form headlines in their ad creative. The lesson: when you have strong brand equity and visual creative, the headline is a wayfinding label, not a persuasion device. For challenger brands (like Motion), headlines need to work harder since brand recognition is lower.

7. Strategic Takeaways

Signals to Watch

  • SKIMS is going all-in on Swim and NikeSKIMS — 271 active ads across just two collections. This is a major seasonal bet with massive budget behind it.
  • Sephora is a UGC machine — nearly 70% video, with demo and talking-head formats dominating. They're running partner brand ads (Glossier, Too Faced, Makeup by Mario) as their own, acting as a platform.
  • Both mega-brands rotate in under a week — this signals they're optimizing for frequency cap management and creative fatigue prevention at scale.
  • SKIMS runs 9% offer-first banners — a promotional tactic that Sephora doesn't lean into. This suggests SKIMS converts on price messaging, not just aspiration.
  • FitBoutique is under-testing — only 24 active ads with repetitive messaging. Opportunity to diversify concepts and formats.

Patterns Worth Borrowing (for Motion)

  • Sephora's UGC demo hook: Product introduction in 0-3 seconds, then show it working. Maps to screen recording demos for SaaS.
  • SKIMS' collection-based campaign structure: Instead of one-off ads, group creatives around a "collection" (feature set, use case). This creates coherent campaigns.
  • Sephora's "New!" prefix pattern: Simple, attention-catching, creates urgency. Works for feature launches.
  • SKIMS' visual-first, copy-second approach: Let the product do the talking. For Motion, this means leading with the dashboard/insight, not the description.
  • Both brands' aggressive rotation: No creative should run more than 7-10 days without refresh. Build a pipeline, not a single hero ad.
